# NICHE — Nail salon / beauty (Queen Nail, Jurong East, Singapore)

**Rule:** the niche's best sites decide the visual language. These references were taken from the HubSpot roundup "25 nail salon website designs we love" (blog.hubspot.com/website/nail-salon-websites, re-checked 2026-11-10) — all live, real salon businesses.

## 1. Park Slope Nails — Brooklyn, NYC
- **URL:** https://parkslopenails.com
- **What makes it work:** One strong brand colour (pink) carried through every CTA, text accent and photo, so the whole site feels cohesive. Clear, strong booking CTAs everywhere including a phone pop-up — the site is built to convert, not just to look pretty. Strong visuals first, copy second.

## 2. MiniLuxe — US chain
- **URL:** https://miniluxe.com
- **What makes it work:** Radical simplicity in design, copy and navigation — a seamless brand experience. Uses simple numbers on the homepage to translate expertise into trust. Clean layout lets the work and the booking flow speak; nothing fights for attention.

## 3. Tropical Popical — bold / playful
- **URL:** https://www.tropicalpopical.com
- **What makes it work:** In a world of soothing spa colours it dares to be different — explosive colour and vibrant imagery that match bold nail art. Custom cursor, fun energy throughout — yet booking and voucher CTAs stay prominent, so the playfulness never costs conversions. Proof a nail site can be loud and still sell.

## 4. Paint Bar — Los Angeles
- **URL:** https://www.paintbarla.com
- **What makes it work:** Breezy minimalism with very little text — headings, imagery and CTAs do the talking. Rotating hero images show the salon interior and the team at work, so visitors can picture the appointment before booking. Restraint as a design choice.

## 5. Le Charme Nail Spa — Brooklyn, NYC
- **URL:** https://www.lecharmenails.com
- **What makes it work:** A slick black-and-white theme — the only site in the roundup with no painted-nail photos at all. Full-width studio photography in the hero sells the *experience* (pick a colour from the wall, relax in the chair), not the end product. Lesson: atmosphere can carry a salon site without a single nail close-up.

## Visual direction chosen for Queen Nail — "LACQUER POP"
Distilled from the references (esp. Tropical Popical's boldness + Park Slope's single-colour discipline + Le Charme's dark base):
- **Palette:** deep aubergine/near-black plum base, hot coral-red + vivid pink as the two accents, warm off-white type. NO ivory paper, NO near-black ink + one accent house template.
- **Type:** chunky rounded grotesque display (huge, tight, uppercase) + clean readable sans for body. Big numbers, big prices-when-known.
- **Layout idiom:** nail-polish swatch chips as a recurring motif — services presented as a wall of colour swatches; gallery as a full-bleed swatch grid; booking CTA as a giant "Book your chair" colour block.
- **Motion personality:** playful and kinetic — swatches pop/slide in with stagger, marquee strip of service names, colour fills on hover. One hero move per moment.
- **Hero moment:** giant "QUEEN NAIL" type over a full-bleed AI-generated nail-art visual, with a coral "Book now" block — colour first, booking second, everything else after.
